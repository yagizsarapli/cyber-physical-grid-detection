## Abstract

Distinguishing a cyber false-data-injection attack (FDIA) from a legitimate physical disturbance, localizing it, and doing so within a protection-relevant time budget are usually studied separately. We study detection and localization across three networks (a synthetic inverter-dominated 5-bus microgrid and two standard IEEE test systems, 14-bus and 30-bus), and separately profile real-time feasibility on the 5-bus implementation, with a weighted-least-squares (WLS) AC state estimator, to test whether explicit topology-relational features (neighbor- and incident-edge-comparison statistics) help. The evaluation enforces strict separation between relational and non-relational feature sets, uses deployable WLS residuals rather than oracle state, and selects models exclusively on validation data. On this pipeline, explicit topology-relational features show no reproducible benefit at 5-bus or IEEE 14-bus for detection (gaps of +0.008±0.012 and -0.003±0.013 against the matched residual-plus-prior ablation, sign-unstable at both), but a gap positive in all four tested seeds at IEEE 30-bus (+0.039±0.029). For localization, the pattern inverts: no reproducible benefit at 5-bus or IEEE 30-bus (-0.007±0.016 and +0.002±0.023, both sign-unstable), but a gap never negative across the same four seeds at IEEE 14-bus (+0.023±0.021). Neither effect is statistically confirmed at this seed count — a 4-seed t-based confidence interval crosses zero for both. Profiling the full decision pipeline against a one-cycle (20 ms at 50 Hz) budget on a documented Apple-arm64/macOS 15.6.1 environment gives, across two independent N=300 reruns, 16.2–17.0 ms median, 16.4–18.2 ms p95, and 17.0–19.2 ms p99 latency, while rare solver-tail maxima reach 89.1–92.7 ms; the detector does not generalize to a completely unseen attack type (0-1.3% recall, vs. 91-100% when that type is in training).

## 1. Introduction

Modernizing power grids toward higher penetrations of inverter-based resources (PV, battery storage, grid-forming converters) changes the character of protection and monitoring in two ways relevant here. First, inverters provide far less fault current than synchronous generators, which strains protection schemes designed around synchronous-machine fault signatures [1]. Second, the same measurement infrastructure used for state awareness is now also an attack surface: an adversary who can spoof a subset of sensor readings can attempt a false-data-injection attack (FDIA) against the state estimator that is, by construction, statistically indistinguishable from a legitimate measurement under a residual-based bad-data test [2].

Renewable-heavy grid infrastructure specifically is not a hypothetical target: in December 2025, a destructive cyberattack against Poland's energy sector — corrupted firmware forced into endless restart cycles across roughly 30 wind and solar farms and a heat-and-power plant, during a cold-weather period, attributed to infrastructure overlapping with the Dragonfly/Static Tundra threat actor — was contained before it caused a blackout affecting an estimated 500,000 people [3]. That incident was an OT/firmware-integrity attack rather than a state-estimation FDIA specifically, but it establishes the same operational stakes this paper's threat model targets.

A control-room operator, or an automated protection scheme, therefore faces a three-part problem for any anomalous reading: *is this a cyberattack or a physical event*, *where is it*, and — if it is to inform a protective action rather than only a post-hoc forensic one — *can a decision be reached inside a protection-relevant time budget*. These three questions are usually studied separately. A large FDIA-detection literature (§2) asks the first question. A smaller attack-localization literature asks the second. Real-time feasibility is addressed by a much smaller, very recent body of work — notably a 2026 benchmark [4] finding deep classifiers individually fast (sub-15ms) but the measured end-to-end pipeline is not (50-90ms), calling this "a critical gap between algorithmic capability and protection-grade deployment." A separate, popular line of work asks whether giving a detector explicit *topology* improves on any of this; that question is this paper's own starting point.

This paper studies detection and localization within a common evaluation framework across three networks, and separately profiles real-time feasibility on the 5-bus implementation. The evaluation framework enforces strict separation between relational and non-relational feature sets, uses deployable WLS residuals rather than oracle state, and selects models exclusively on validation data. We independently reproduce the qualitative pipeline-latency finding of concurrent work [4] (fast classifiers embedded in a slow end-to-end pipeline) and localize the actual bottleneck: not the classifier, not the state estimator's Newton-type iteration count, but a measurement-table object being rebuilt from scratch through a general-purpose element-creation API every cycle, when its structure never changes — fixing that one thing accounts for most of the gap closed.

### Contributions
- A task- and network-specific result across three networks: explicit topology-relational features show no reproducible detection benefit at 5-bus or IEEE 14-bus, but a gap positive in all four tested seeds at IEEE 30-bus; for localization, the pattern inverts — no reproducible benefit at 5-bus or IEEE 30-bus, but a gap never negative across the same four seeds at IEEE 14-bus (§4, §7; neither effect is statistically confirmed at this seed count) — together with a structural, quantitative redundancy analysis that rules out one natural explanation for why the two standard IEEE systems disagree with the custom microgrid.
- A root-cause profiling methodology for the "fast model, slow pipeline" gap identified in prior work, and a demonstrated fix that closes most of it without hardware acceleration (§5).
- A further explicit negative result reported as a scope condition rather than omitted: no zero-day attack-type generalization (§6).

## 2. Related Work

### Foundational FDIA and Stealth Constructions The FDIA threat model here — an attacker with knowledge of the network model constructing a measurement perturbation that leaves the WLS residual unchanged — follows Liu, Ning & Reiter's foundational construction [2] and its AC-consistent extensions [5]. A growing subline constructs such attacks with *less* attacker knowledge: without line parameters [6], or fully "blind" to topology using generative/physics-informed models [7,8]. Low-magnitude attacks close to the sensor noise floor have their own dedicated detection literature [9].

### Deep-Learning FDIA Detection A substantial literature applies CNN/LSTM/transfer-learning architectures to FDIA detection [10,11,12], generally evaluated as binary classification on a fixed attack distribution. This project's `residual_only`/`prior_only` baselines sit in this tradition; the contribution is isolating what *explicit topology-relational* features add beyond a fair combination of the two, under stress-tested conditions and features/model-selection that are actually deployable.

### Topology-Aware / Graph-Based State Estimation and Detection The closest published framing is topology-aware GNN-based state estimation [13,14,15,16]. Checking the closest of these directly — full text, not abstracts — shows a mixed picture: [13] and an earlier related study [14] both compare their GNN against a same-feature "regular" DNN baseline — a genuine, correctly-scoped topology ablation; [15]'s non-graph baseline is not clearly confirmed to receive identical input features on the text available. We do *not* claim this specific ablation is generally absent from the literature — in at least two cases it is not. This paper's contribution is narrower: a same-architecture, same-feature comparison checks whether a different *model* needs topology; it does not by itself catch a failure mode one level lower, in how the "non-topological" feature *set* is defined, or whether the features/selection procedure are actually deployable — a distinct failure mode this paper's own methodology (§3) is built to guard against. §4's out-of-fold linear-redundancy check is offered as a second, cheaper pre-check for the feature-leakage variant of this failure mode.

### Attack/Event Localization Localization work spans capsule networks for load-altering attacks across 14/39/57-bus systems [17], moving-target-defense-assisted localization [18], and online detection-plus-localization schemes [19]. None of the abstracts available report a localization method's sensitivity to network topology/meshedness directly, or an ablation of relational features against a matched non-relational baseline — exactly the comparison this paper's §4 and §7 run.

### Real-Time / Latency-Aware Classification [4] is the direct comparison point for §5 — it independently measures the same qualitative gap on a different, larger testbed using an industrial EMT simulator and recommends hardware acceleration, where this paper instead finds and fixes a software-level cause for a comparable fraction of the gap.

### Zero-Day / Unseen-Attack Generalization The general intrusion-detection literature widely reports that supervised detectors do not reliably generalize to unseen attack families; a verified, quantitative comparison specific to power-systems FDIA remains an open point for future work.

### Field-Wide Self-Assessment of Adjacent Gaps A 2025 scoping review of ML in power-system protection [20] independently reports two gaps this paper runs into directly: most reviewed studies don't discuss latency/real-time constraints at all (§5), and cross-topology generalization is essentially untested field-wide (§7).

## 3. Testbed and Methodology

**Network.** A 5-bus, 0.4kV synthetic microgrid built in `pandapower`: point of common coupling, a PV bus modeled as a steady-state grid-following (GFL) power injection, a BESS bus modeled as a steady-state grid-forming (GFM) power injection, and two load buses, connected by 4 lines. Both are static `sgen` operating-point injections, not dynamic converter-control models — no droop control or virtual inertia is simulated; the GFL/GFM labels describe each device's intended role, not modeled control dynamics. `net.f_hz = 50.0` (verified, not assumed — fixes the protection-relevant budget to 20ms).

**State estimation.** WLS AC state estimation via `pandapower.estimation.estimate`, 23 measurements per cycle. All "residual" features throughout this paper (5-bus, IEEE 14-bus, IEEE 30-bus alike) are the standard, deployable normalized measurement residual (z - h(x̂))/σ computed from the WLS solution — never a comparison to a ground-truth state a real detector could not observe.

**Threat model.** Two synthetic attack families, both targeting a randomly chosen load bus per scenario: *Naive* (a single voltage-magnitude sensor spoofed by a fixed-range random offset) and *Model-consistent ("stealth") FDIA* (Liu-Ning-Reiter construction adapted to the AC case; the attack vector a = h(x_attack) - h(x_true) is computed exactly, so it is invisible to the chi-square residual test by construction). A benign hard-negative case (`physical_load_disturbance`, 25-70% load step) is included specifically to be confusable with an attack.

**The `prior_only` feature set, and exactly how its prior is constructed.** Every replication starts from one randomly sampled base operating point, shared across that replication's 4 scenarios. Before any attack/disturbance is applied, an independent copy of that same base point has its loads perturbed by a fresh, independent forecast error (N(0, 2.5%) per load at 5-bus, clipped to ±6%; N(0, 5%) at IEEE 14-bus/30-bus — a cross-network noise-model difference documented as a limitation below) and solved through its own power flow — this is the "prior." `prior_only` features are the deviation between the WLS estimate and this prior. The prior is constructed *before*, and entirely independently of, whichever scenario the true network becomes.

**Features.** Four feature sets are compared. `residual_only`: conventional WLS measurement residuals. `prior_only`: the prior-deviation features defined above. `residual_plus_prior`: the two combined, with no relational or structural information at all — the topology-free ablation. `topology_fusion`: `residual_plus_prior` plus explicit relational features describing each bus relative to its network neighbors (local-minus-neighbor innovation/residual, incident-edge aggregates); no static structural metadata (node degree, device role) beyond that, since neither is a scenario-specific neighbor comparison and both risk leaking attack-target eligibility (the attack generator above only ever targets load buses). An earlier pass of this pipeline defined `residual_only`/`prior_only` by column-name substring matching, which let relational columns leak into the non-relational baselines, and separately let node degree and device-role flags reach every feature set including `topology_fusion`; both were corrected — by excluding columns matching explicit relational-name markers, and by removing structural metadata outright — before any result in this paper.

**Models.** LogisticRegression, RandomForest, and HistGradientBoosting classifiers are compared with replication-group-safe 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits and group-aware bootstrap confidence intervals. Model selection uses the validation split exclusively: each candidate model is scored on validation, the best-scoring model per feature set is selected, and only that model's (until then unseen) test-set score is reported.

**Sample-size terminology.** A *replication* is one independently sampled base operating point; each yields exactly 4 *scenarios* (clean, naive-attack, stealth-attack, physical-disturbance), so N replications yield 4N scenarios. The 70/15/15 split is applied at the replication level. The headline 5-bus result uses N=500 replications → 2000 scenarios → ≈300 test scenarios; §7's IEEE 14-bus/30-bus results use the same N=500 → 300 test scenarios for detection, 150 for localization, at both networks.

## 4. Results I: The Topology-Fusion Advantage Does Not Survive Its Own Ablation, at Either Task

Attack magnitude is hardened roughly 3x toward the sensor noise floor, relative to the naive detection regime a first pass would produce, and evaluated at n=300 test scenarios (§3). `prior_only` alone is missing residual information that `topology_fusion` has access to, so a comparison against `prior_only` specifically cannot, by construction, distinguish "topology helped" from "having residual information at all helped"; the fair comparison is against the topology-free `residual_plus_prior` ablation instead.

### Primary Ablation Table 1 compares `topology_fusion` against `residual_plus_prior` (§3), for the primary seed and both stress conditions.

**Table 1 — topology_fusion vs. the topology-free ablation (best validation-selected model per cell; 5-bus, n=300 detection / n=150 cyber-only localization test scenarios)**

| Condition | Detection: topo. fusion | Detection: res.+prior | Localization: topo. fusion | Localization: res.+prior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hard | 0.950 | 0.937 | 0.967 | 0.967 |
| Harder (+2x noise) | 0.950 | 0.947 | 0.960 | 0.967 |

Detection shows a small edge for `topology_fusion` at this one seed, in both conditions (+0.013 Hard, +0.003 Harder); localization is an exact tie under Hard and a small edge for `residual_plus_prior` under Harder (-0.007). Neither column is bolded here — a single seed's small, direction-inconsistent gap is not evidence of a real winner, and the four-seed replication directly below (which includes this same seed) is the test that actually matters. For reference, both feature sets still comfortably beat `prior_only` alone (0.860-0.870 detection, 0.687-0.693 localization) and `residual_only` alone (0.700 detection, 0.547 localization) — the gain over either single baseline is large; it is simply not a topology effect.

![Topology_fusion vs. the topology-free residual+prior ablation vs. prior_only alone, across 4 seeds](figures/paper_fig1_multiseed_advantage.png)

**The null result replicates across independent random seeds, on both tasks.** Repeating full data generation and evaluation for four independent seeds, with localization training restricted to cyber-scenario nodes only (matching the convention used at IEEE 14-bus/30-bus, §7), gives a `topology_fusion`-vs-`residual_plus_prior` detection gap of +0.008±0.012 (range -0.010 to +0.017) and a localization gap of -0.007±0.016 (range -0.020 to +0.013, including one exact tie): both cross zero and change sign seed to seed, consistent with sampling variability around a near-zero effect in either direction, on either task.

### Redundancy Diagnostic Predicting each of the 65 relational columns from `residual_plus_prior` alone, with 5-fold cross-validated ridge regression, gives a mean out-of-fold R² of 0.956 (median 1.000, minimum 0.613, 90.8% of columns above 0.8): a neighbor-mean or incident-edge aggregate over a fixed, small neighbor set is, on a network this size, close to an affine function of the same per-bus values `residual_plus_prior` already provides in full. A sufficiently flexible classifier may therefore reconstruct much of that relational information internally without ever being given it explicitly — consistent with Table 1's near-ties on both tasks. This explanation is specific to a fixed, small topology, and does not by itself predict what happens at larger, standard networks (§7) — reduced recoverability of relational features from local information remains one possible condition under which explicit topology-awareness might help; §7 tests whether such benefits appear on larger networks, while the Discussion examines whether reduced redundancy explains the observed pattern.

## 5. Results II: Real-Time Feasibility — Diagnosing and Fixing the Gap

Following [4]'s finding that fast classifiers can sit inside a slow end-to-end pipeline, we measured the full per-cycle decision pipeline against a one-cycle (20ms at the network's confirmed 50Hz) target, with one-time setup excluded.

### Initial Measurement Median 42.98ms, 2.15x over budget (RandomForest, tolerance=1e-7, maximum_iterations=40, flat init every cycle).

### Warm-Start Test Warm-starting produced only a 1.06x speedup (37.14ms).

### Profiling The measurement-submission step cost 19.9ms — essentially the same as the estimator's own solve (19.4ms). An iteration-count ablation showed the true requirement is ~4-5 steps regardless of the 40-iteration cap.

### Measurement-Update Optimization The measurement table was rebuilt from scratch every cycle via a general-purpose API, even though its structure never changes. A one-time construction + in-place overwrite gave a 1158x speedup on this sub-step (19.4ms → 0.017ms), verified bit-identical (max difference 0.0).

### Final Benchmark With a lighter classifier (LogisticRegression), two independent N=300 reruns on the same documented environment, both with 0 convergence failures, give 16.155–17.015ms median, 16.429–18.211ms p95, 16.952–19.190ms p99, and 89.067–92.657ms max end-to-end latency. Median, p95, and p99 remain within the 20ms budget in both reruns; the maximum exceeds it in both. Across those reruns, stage medians are 15.855–16.664ms for WLS state estimation, 0.050–0.057ms for post-estimation residual/innovation calculation, 0.083–0.088ms for feature computation, 0.0068–0.0076ms for array assembly, and 0.160–0.200ms for inference, so the WLS solve dominates the budget. The documented benchmark environment is Apple arm64 / macOS 15.6.1, Python 3.12.4, pandapower 2.14.10, NumPy 1.26.4, pandas 2.2.2, and scikit-learn 1.4.2. Feature computation is a small fraction of the total either way — state estimation, not the feature set or classifier, is what the budget is spent on. The classifier is trained on 150 real warm-up scenarios generated through the same simulated pipeline (83 attack, 67 clean; training balanced accuracy 1.000), and the residual/innovation features use the same deployable quantities as everywhere else; the timer covers all five pipeline stages end to end (state estimation, post-estimation residual/innovation calculation, feature computation, array assembly, inference), so the headline numbers above are the true total, nothing excluded.

### Positioning [4] measures the same category of gap on an industrial EMT simulator and recommends hardware acceleration; this paper's finding suggests pipeline profiling deserves consideration first. A knowledge-distilled LightGBM detector for virtualized microgrids reports ~0.06ms/sample for inference alone [22] — the same order of magnitude as this paper's own inference sub-step (~0.16–0.20ms/sample), far below the state-estimation sub-step (~15.9–16.7ms/sample).

## 6. Results III: The Advantage Does Not Extrapolate to Unseen Attack Types

To test generalization beyond the two trained attack families, we trained HistGradientBoosting — with the same tuned hyperparameters as the reported 5-bus detector elsewhere in this paper — with one entire family withheld, then measured recall on that family, evaluated against the standard test split under a symmetric protocol (the held-out and seen-in-training conditions are compared on the same kind of row). Run for both `topology_fusion` and `residual_plus_prior`.

**Table 2 — Recall on a withheld vs. trained-on attack type (n=75 test scenarios per cell, both conditions)**

| Attack type | Features | Withheld | In training |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naive corruption | topo. fusion | 0.000 | 0.907 |
| Naive corruption | res.+prior | 0.000 | 0.907 |
| Stealth FDIA | topo. fusion | 0.013 | 1.000 |
| Stealth FDIA | res.+prior | 0.013 | 1.000 |

![Recall collapses when an attack type is withheld from training](figures/paper_fig3_zeroday_generalization.png)

The detector recognizes attack types in its training data very well but does not extrapolate to a mechanism it has not seen, identically whether or not topology-relational features are included — the two feature sets are indistinguishable on every cell. We report this as a hard scope limit: within this project's own two-family taxonomy, this is a complete absence of zero-day capability, not a partial one, for either feature set. This complements a related gap in the fault-classification literature: an XGBoost classifier trained/evaluated on the *same* distribution still drops from 96.8% (synthetic) to 85.2% (physical) data [23] — even without an unseen attack type, a synthetic-to-real shift alone costs over 11 points.

## 7. Results IV: Cross-Network Evaluation Reveals Task- and Network-Specific Effects

Everything in §4-§6 uses the custom 5-bus microgrid. We separately tested explicit topology-relational features at a larger, standard scale, using a completely independent, size-agnostic feature-engineering design.

### Test-System Selection IEEE 14-bus and IEEE 30-bus were selected because their `pandapower` bus indexing maps directly to the internal power-flow representation used by the evaluation pipeline (confirmed directly), and they are among the most commonly used benchmarks in the state-estimation/FDIA literature.

### Cross-Network Method All WLS/measurement/attack machinery is network-size-agnostic and reused unmodified. Fixed per-bus feature columns were replaced with a size-agnostic design: global summary statistics of the residual/innovation arrays, plus a trained per-node classifier for localization. This feature design is implemented separately from §3's, but corrects for the same three issues, for the same reasons: the residual feature is the deployable measurement residual z - h(x̂) (never the simulator's hidden ground-truth state), model selection is validation-based rather than test-set-based, and `topology_fusion` excludes both feature-name-collision leakage into the non-relational baselines and static structural metadata (node degree) — degree could otherwise act as a static target-eligibility prior, since attacks here, too, only ever target load buses. All numbers below are from this corrected pipeline, re-run in full at N=500 replications, four seeds, at both networks.

### Cross-Network Result At IEEE 14-bus, the detection gap changes sign across seeds (mean -0.003±0.013) — null, matching 5-bus. The localization gap does not: non-negative in all 4 seeds (mean +0.023±0.021, three seeds strictly positive and one exact tie), never favoring residual_plus_prior. At IEEE 30-bus, the pattern inverts entirely: the detection gap is positive in all 4 seeds (mean +0.039±0.029) — roughly 1.7x IEEE-14's localization effect. The localization gap at IEEE-30 changes sign across seeds (mean +0.002±0.023) — null, matching 5-bus.

### Statistical Caveat Four seeds is enough to see whether a gap's sign is stable, but not enough, on its own, to call an effect statistically confirmed. A standard t-based 95% confidence interval on the IEEE-30 detection gap is +0.039 ± 0.047 (i.e. [-0.008, 0.086]), and on the IEEE-14 localization gap is +0.023 ± 0.033 (i.e. [-0.010, 0.056]) — both cross zero. A distribution-free sign test gives a one-sided p=0.5⁴=0.0625 for the IEEE-30 detection gap (four of four seeds strictly positive); the IEEE-14 localization gap includes one exact tie (Table 4), which a sign test conventionally excludes rather than counts as a positive, leaving three of three non-zero seeds positive and p=0.5³=0.125 — weaker evidence than IEEE-30's, not the same figure. Neither reaches the conventional 0.05 threshold. We therefore describe both findings throughout this paper as *consistent in sign across all four tested seeds*, not as a statistically confirmed effect: the evidence is that every seed drawn so far points the same way (or ties, never against it), which is meaningfully different from, and weaker than, a claim that the underlying effect is confirmed nonzero. The sign test's own bar is low if the pattern holds — a fifth same-direction seed at IEEE-30 would already cross one-sided p<0.05 (0.5⁵=0.03125) — so the 10–20-seed estimate below targets a harder goal instead: a tight confidence interval on the effect's *magnitude*, not sign-test significance. Resolving that would need those additional seeds or a paired/bootstrap difference test; neither was run here, and we flag the gap explicitly rather than let the stronger-sounding language stand uncorrected.

### Interpretation With everything corrected, the result is: *detection* shows no reproducible advantage at 5-bus or IEEE-14, but a gap positive in all four tested seeds at IEEE-30; *localization* shows no reproducible advantage at 5-bus or IEEE-30, but a gap never negative across the same four seeds at IEEE-14. Neither task is uniform across networks, and the two consistent-sign gaps that do survive do not co-occur at the same network or match any single earlier hypothesis this project raised along the way ("topology helps everywhere," "topology helps nowhere," "topology helps localization specifically, and only at larger networks"). This sharpens a 2025 field-wide scoping review's [20] independent finding that cross-topology generalization is essentially untested across the literature it surveyed.

## 8. Discussion

Read together, §4-§7 converge on a finding that is uniform for neither task, reached across three networks and independently-written pipelines. For *detection*: explicit topology-relational features show no reproducible advantage over a matched non-relational ablation at 5-bus (a null, sign-unstable gap, mean +0.008±0.012) or IEEE 14-bus (a null, sign-unstable gap, mean -0.003±0.013) — but a gap positive in all four tested seeds at IEEE 30-bus (mean +0.039±0.029). For *localization*: no reproducible advantage at 5-bus (a null, sign-unstable gap, mean -0.007±0.016) or IEEE 30-bus (a null, sign-unstable gap) — but a gap never negative across the same four seeds at IEEE 14-bus (mean +0.023±0.021). Put plainly: each of the two standard IEEE test systems shows a topology-favoring gap that holds its sign across all four tested seeds in exactly one of the two tasks, and it is a *different* task at each network; the small custom 5-bus microgrid shows no such gap in either task. As stated above, four same-signed seeds is consistent-sign evidence, not a statistically confirmed effect — a t-based confidence interval at this sample size crosses zero for both gaps, and neither should be read as more settled than that.

What helps detection at 5-bus, considerably, is combining residual and prior-innovation information — not a topology-specific effect. §4's redundancy analysis gives the 5-bus null results (now on both tasks) a structural footing: mean out-of-fold R² = 0.956, so there is little left for an explicit relational feature to contribute on a fixed, small topology. The natural hypothesis — that this redundancy weakens on a bigger, more meshed network, explaining IEEE-14's localization reversal — is directly testable, so we tested it: repeating the identical out-of-fold check on IEEE-14's own relational localization features gives a mean R² of 0.966 (median 0.999, 92% above 0.8) — if anything *higher* than the 5-bus figure, not lower. Reduced linear recoverability does not explain the IEEE-14 localization pattern under this diagnostic. We have not run the equivalent check for IEEE-30's detection-relevant relational features — unlike the localization case, where the same node-level design transfers directly between 5-bus and IEEE-14, IEEE-30's detection features and 5-bus's use different, non-comparable representations, and building a third variant of this diagnostic was judged out of scope. Whether redundancy explains, or fails to explain, IEEE-30's detection advantage is accordingly an open question, not a tested one — we are explicit that we do not know the answer.

One plausible explanation for the IEEE-14 case, though not directly verified, is discoverability rather than availability: a tree-based model with no built-in notion of "neighbor" must find the right subset of a flat input to combine for each node, a search that may get harder with more nodes even when the information is linearly present throughout. This remains genuinely open, and the picture is now more complicated than a single "discoverability scales with size" story would predict: IEEE-30 is larger than IEEE-14 by node count, yet its localization story (if that is what IEEE-14's is) does not extend to it at all — IEEE-30's own localization gap is null — while IEEE-30 instead shows an advantage in the *other* task, detection, which 5-bus and IEEE-14 both show no advantage in. A mechanism predicting discoverability difficulty scaling smoothly and uniformly across tasks does not fit this pattern; identifying what does is future work.

The more important finding here is not a scale boundary on a single confirmed effect, but a genuinely task- and network-specific pattern with two distinct, consistent-sign gaps and no uniform story connecting them.

## 9. Limitations

1. **Scale and network-specificity**: Three network topologies have now been tested to the same 4-seed standard. Detection shows a gap consistently positive across all four tested seeds at exactly one of them (IEEE-30); localization at exactly one, different, network (IEEE-14), never negative across the same four seeds. None uses a real (non-synthetic) network or load profile, and with only three networks tested, the emerging pattern (each standard IEEE system favoring a different task) is a two-data-point observation, not something a fourth or fifth network is guaranteed to extend in either direction.
2. **Four seeds is consistent-sign evidence, not a statistically confirmed effect**: both surviving gaps (IEEE-30 detection, IEEE-14 localization) are positive/non-negative in all four tested seeds, but a standard t-based 95% confidence interval crosses zero for both (+0.039±0.047 and +0.023±0.033 respectively). A distribution-free sign test does not reach p<0.05 for either: one-sided p=0.0625 for IEEE-30 (four of four seeds positive), and p=0.125 for IEEE-14 once its one exact tie is excluded per sign-test convention (three of three non-zero seeds positive, not four of four). We describe both findings as consistent in sign across every seed tested, deliberately short of claiming statistical confirmation; one further same-direction seed would cross the sign test's own 0.05 threshold at IEEE-30, but a stable estimate of either effect's magnitude would need roughly 10–20 seeds or a paired/bootstrap difference test, neither run here.
3. **Cross-network measurement-noise and forecast-uncertainty mismatch**: measurement standard deviations are fixed absolute values shared, unscaled, across all three networks despite their very different physical scales, and prior load-forecast uncertainty is 2.5% at 5-bus vs. 5% at IEEE-14/IEEE-30 (§3). This was not standardized or swept as an independent robustness axis; §7's cross-network comparisons should be read with this confound explicitly in mind, not as a controlled scale-only experiment. In light of this, "network-specific" throughout this paper is more precisely *setup-and-network-specific under the tested measurement/prior regimes*: each network's result reflects its topology together with its own noise/prior configuration, not topology in isolation. IEEE-14 and IEEE-30 share an identical noise/prior convention, so their disagreement with each other (one task's gap each, not the same task) is not subject to this particular confound; only comparisons that include 5-bus are.
4. **No zero-day generalization** (§6): numbers hold only for attack types represented in training — verified for both feature sets, which collapse identically, under a corrected, symmetric evaluation protocol; localization was not separately re-tested for zero-day generalization, and the attack taxonomy is still limited to two families (item 5).
5. **Single testbed family, two attack types**: the present AC-consistent attack generator does not independently vary the compromised-measurement subset; introducing explicit attacker-access constraints as a separate, tunable parameter is left for future work.
6. **Latency results** (§5) characterize one documented Apple-arm64/macOS 15.6.1 Python/`pandapower` environment, not a hardware/RTOS claim, and were not re-benchmarked on the smaller `residual_plus_prior` feature set (argued, not re-measured, not to change the conclusion).

## 10. Conclusion

Across a small custom inverter-dominated microgrid and two standard, larger IEEE test systems (14-bus and 30-bus), explicit topology-relational features show no uniform advantage for either detection or localization. What they show instead is a strictly task-and-network-specific pattern: a detection gap consistently positive across all four tested seeds at IEEE 30-bus only (never at 5-bus or IEEE 14-bus), and a localization gap never negative across the same four seeds at IEEE 14-bus only (never at 5-bus or IEEE 30-bus) — both, we state explicitly, consistent-sign evidence rather than a statistically confirmed effect: a 4-seed confidence interval crosses zero for either gap on its own, and resolving that properly would need substantially more seeds than this paper reports. We take this as a caution against generalizing any single network's result — in either direction, for either task — into a general rule from one or two data points. Separately, the resulting decision pipeline, verified with a trained classifier and deployable features throughout, can be brought within a one-cycle target once its actual bottleneck, a software inefficiency, is fixed, and the detector does not extend to attack types unseen in training.
