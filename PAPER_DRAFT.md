## Abstract

Distinguishing a cyber false-data-injection attack (FDIA) from a legitimate physical disturbance, localizing it, and doing so within a protection-relevant time budget are usually studied separately. We study detection and localization across three networks (a synthetic inverter-dominated 5-bus microgrid and two standard IEEE test systems, 14-bus and 30-bus), and separately profile real-time feasibility on the 5-bus implementation, with a weighted-least-squares (WLS) AC state estimator, built specifically to test whether explicit topology-relational features (neighbor- and incident-edge-comparison statistics) help. A first pass on the 5-bus testbed appeared to show they did: `topology_fusion` beat a prior-based baseline at both detection (0.950 vs. 0.873) and localization (0.980 vs. 0.780), confirmed across four seeds. That comparison, and two further problems found only in a subsequent, deliberately adversarial audit of our own pipeline, were not fair ones. First, at 5-bus: topology-relational columns are named after the residual/innovation quantities they compare, and a name-substring-based split let roughly half of them leak into what were meant to be topology-free baselines; separately, those same baselines also carried node degree and device-role flags that reveal which buses are even attack-eligible. Second, independently, at IEEE 14-bus and 30-bus: the "residual" feature used the simulator's hidden ground-truth state rather than the deployable WLS measurement residual, and model selection picked whichever of three candidate models scored best on the test set itself rather than on a held-out validation split — the same test-set-selection bug, more disguised, was also present at 5-bus. Correcting all of this and re-running every result at every network across four independent seeds gives a materially different, and more interesting, finding than any single pass along the way suggested. For *detection*, explicit topology-relational features show no reproducible benefit at 5-bus or IEEE 14-bus (gaps of +0.003±0.010 and +0.002±0.013 against the best non-relational alternative, sign-unstable at both) — but a real, 4-seed-consistent advantage emerges at IEEE 30-bus (+0.039±0.032, never negative), a finding present in no earlier version of this analysis. For *localization*, the pattern inverts: no reproducible benefit at 5-bus or IEEE 30-bus (+0.005±0.036 and +0.008±0.018, both sign-unstable), but a real advantage at IEEE 14-bus (+0.018±0.021, never negative across the four seeds). Neither task shows a uniform answer, and each of the two effects that do survive appears at a different one of the two standard IEEE systems, not at both and not at the custom microgrid. Two further, independent results hold up essentially unchanged under the same audit: profiling the full decision pipeline — now using a classifier trained on genuine warm-up scenarios and deployable features throughout, not a placeholder — against a one-cycle (20 ms at 50 Hz) budget still brings it to 16.9/17.6/18.4 ms at the median/p95/p99, and the detector still does not generalize to a completely unseen attack type (0-1.3% recall, vs. 91-100% when that type is in training). We report this corrected, considerably more complicated three-network, two-task result, obtained only by auditing and re-auditing our own pipeline rather than trusting an earlier, more publication-friendly pass through it.

## 1. Introduction

Modernizing power grids toward higher penetrations of inverter-based resources (PV, battery storage, grid-forming converters) changes the character of protection and monitoring in two ways relevant here. First, inverters provide far less fault current than synchronous generators, which strains protection schemes designed around synchronous-machine fault signatures [1]. Second, the same measurement infrastructure used for state awareness is now also an attack surface: an adversary who can spoof a subset of sensor readings can attempt a false-data-injection attack (FDIA) against the state estimator that is, by construction, statistically indistinguishable from a legitimate measurement under a residual-based bad-data test [2].

Renewable-heavy grid infrastructure specifically is not a hypothetical target: in December 2025, a destructive cyberattack against Poland's energy sector — corrupted firmware forced into endless restart cycles across roughly 30 wind and solar farms and a heat-and-power plant, during a cold-weather period, attributed to infrastructure overlapping with the Dragonfly/Static Tundra threat actor — was contained before it caused a blackout affecting an estimated 500,000 people [3]. That incident was an OT/firmware-integrity attack rather than a state-estimation FDIA specifically, but it establishes the same operational stakes this paper's threat model targets.

A control-room operator, or an automated protection scheme, therefore faces a three-part problem for any anomalous reading: *is this a cyberattack or a physical event*, *where is it*, and — if it is to inform a protective action rather than only a post-hoc forensic one — *can a decision be reached inside a protection-relevant time budget*. These three questions are usually studied separately. A large FDIA-detection literature (§2) asks the first question. A smaller attack-localization literature asks the second. Real-time feasibility is addressed by a much smaller, very recent body of work — notably a 2026 benchmark [4] finding deep classifiers individually fast (sub-15ms) but the measured end-to-end pipeline is not (50-90ms), calling this "a critical gap between algorithmic capability and protection-grade deployment." A separate, popular line of work asks whether giving a detector explicit *topology* improves on any of this; that question is this paper's own starting point.

This paper studies detection and localization within a common evaluation framework across three networks, and separately profiles real-time feasibility on the 5-bus implementation — and, in the process of doing so honestly, surfaces four corrections to its own early results:

1. **A near-perfect early result was wrong, and wrong for a boring reason.** An initial evaluation of topology-aware detection reported 1.000 balanced accuracy across every metric and three different model families, on a 72-sample test set. Not a data-leakage bug (checked, fixed a real but inconsequential defensive gap, result didn't change) — the attack scenarios were simply too easy. Hardening the attack magnitude initially *reversed* the finding at small sample size (n=72) before a larger run (n=300) showed the reversal was itself noise.
2. **The resulting "topology helps" finding did not survive its own ablation — at 5-bus, then independently at IEEE 14-bus.** `topology_fusion` was compared against `residual_only` and `prior_only`, but not against their union — the actual, fair control. Building that control exposed a feature-labeling bug at 5-bus: relational columns are named after the residual/innovation quantities they compare (e.g. `neighbor_mean_residual`), so a name-substring split let about half of them leak into what were meant to be topology-free baselines. Auditing the separately-implemented IEEE 14-bus pipeline the same way found an analogous flaw — an explicit list inclusion, not a substring collision, but the same problem.
3. **The pipeline-latency gap reported in concurrent work is, at least here, mostly fixable software engineering, not an algorithmic ceiling.** We independently reproduce [4]'s qualitative finding (fast model, slow pipeline) and localize the actual bottleneck: a measurement-table object rebuilt from scratch every cycle, when its structure never changes.
4. **A subsequent, deliberately adversarial audit of our own pipeline found three more problems — and one of them changed the paper's central finding again.** After item 2 appeared to settle into a stable, publication-ready three-network story, a full code-level review of every script behind this paper found: IEEE 14-bus/30-bus's "residual" feature was computed from the simulator's hidden ground-truth state, not the deployable measurement residual the shared estimation code already produced; model selection at all three networks picked whichever of three candidate models scored best on the test set itself, not a held-out validation split; and 5-bus's "topology-free" localization baselines secretly carried node degree and device-role flags. Fixing all three and re-running everything changed the three-network picture a second time — §7 has the full account.

**Contributions:**
- A methodologically rigorous, task- and network-specific result, reached only after two independent rounds of self-auditing: once feature-labeling bugs, an oracle-leaked residual feature, and test-set model selection are all corrected, explicit topology-relational features show no reproducible detection benefit at 5-bus or IEEE 14-bus, but a real, 4-seed-consistent advantage at IEEE 30-bus; for localization, the pattern inverts — no benefit at 5-bus or IEEE 30-bus, but a real advantage at IEEE 14-bus (§4, §7) — together with a structural redundancy analysis that rules out one natural explanation for why the two standard IEEE systems disagree with the custom microgrid.
- A root-cause profiling methodology for the "fast model, slow pipeline" gap identified in prior work, and a demonstrated fix, verified with a genuinely trained detector on deployable features rather than a timing placeholder (§5).
- A further explicit negative result reported as a scope condition: no zero-day attack-type generalization, confirmed under a corrected, symmetric evaluation protocol (§6).

## 2. Related Work

**Foundational FDIA and stealth constructions.** The FDIA threat model here — an attacker with knowledge of the network model constructing a measurement perturbation that leaves the WLS residual unchanged — follows Liu, Ning & Reiter's foundational construction [2] and its AC-consistent extensions [5]. A growing subline constructs such attacks with *less* attacker knowledge: without line parameters [6], or fully "blind" to topology using generative/physics-informed models [7,8]. Low-magnitude attacks close to the sensor noise floor have their own dedicated detection literature [9].

**Deep-learning FDIA detection.** A substantial literature applies CNN/LSTM/transfer-learning architectures to FDIA detection [10,11,12], generally evaluated as binary classification on a fixed attack distribution. This project's `residual_only`/`prior_only` baselines sit in this tradition; the contribution is isolating what *explicit topology-relational* features add beyond a fair combination of the two, under stress-tested conditions and features/model-selection that are actually deployable.

**Topology-aware / graph-based state estimation and detection.** The closest published framing is topology-aware GNN-based state estimation [13,14,15,16]. Checking the closest of these directly — full text, specifically to avoid the kind of overclaim this paper has already had to correct in itself — shows a mixed picture: [13] and an earlier related study [14] both compare their GNN against a same-feature "regular" DNN baseline — a genuine, correctly-scoped topology ablation, of exactly the kind this paper's own original comparison lacked; [15]'s non-graph baseline is not clearly confirmed to receive identical input features on the text available. We do *not* claim this specific ablation is generally absent from the literature — in at least two cases it is not. This paper's contribution is narrower: a same-architecture, same-feature comparison checks whether a different *model* needs topology; it does not by itself catch a bug living one level lower, in how the "non-topological" feature *set* is defined, or whether the features/selection procedure are actually deployable. This paper's own two-round audit (§3, §7) found exactly that kind of problem three separate times, in three separately-written pipelines — undetectable by an architecture-level ablation alone. §4's out-of-fold linear-redundancy check is offered as a second, cheaper pre-check for the feature-leakage variant of this failure mode.

**Attack/event localization.** Localization work spans capsule networks for load-altering attacks across 14/39/57-bus systems [17], moving-target-defense-assisted localization [18], and online detection-plus-localization schemes [19]. None of the abstracts available report a localization method's sensitivity to network topology/meshedness directly, or an ablation of relational features against a same-information non-relational baseline — exactly the comparison this paper's §4 and §7 run.

**Real-time / latency-aware classification.** [4] is the direct comparison point for §5 — it independently measures the same qualitative gap on a different, larger testbed using an industrial EMT simulator and recommends hardware acceleration, where this paper instead finds and fixes a software-level cause for a comparable fraction of the gap.

**Zero-day / unseen-attack generalization.** The general intrusion-detection literature widely reports that supervised detectors do not reliably generalize to unseen attack families; a verified, quantitative comparison figure specific to power-systems FDIA is still outstanding work.

**Field-wide self-assessment of adjacent gaps.** A 2025 scoping review of ML in power-system protection [20] independently reports two gaps this paper runs into directly: most reviewed studies don't discuss latency/real-time constraints at all (§5), and cross-topology generalization is essentially untested field-wide (§7).

## 3. Testbed and Methodology

**Network.** A 5-bus, 0.4kV synthetic microgrid built in `pandapower`: point of common coupling, a PV bus modeled as a steady-state grid-following (GFL) power injection, a BESS bus modeled as a steady-state grid-forming (GFM) power injection, and two load buses, connected by 4 lines. Both are static `sgen` operating-point injections, not dynamic converter-control models — no droop control or virtual inertia is simulated; the GFL/GFM labels describe each device's intended role, not modeled control dynamics. `net.f_hz = 50.0` (verified, not assumed — fixes the protection-relevant budget to 20ms).

**State estimation.** WLS AC state estimation via `pandapower.estimation.estimate`, 23 measurements per cycle. All "residual" features throughout this paper (5-bus, IEEE 14-bus, IEEE 30-bus alike) are the standard, deployable normalized measurement residual (z - h(x̂))/σ computed from the WLS solution — never a comparison to a ground-truth state a real detector could not observe; §7 documents a case where an earlier version of this project's own code violated that rule and how it was caught and fixed.

**Threat model.** Two synthetic attack families, both targeting a randomly chosen load bus per scenario: *Naive* (a single voltage-magnitude sensor spoofed by a fixed-range random offset) and *Model-consistent ("stealth") FDIA* (Liu-Ning-Reiter construction adapted to the AC case; the attack vector a = h(x_attack) - h(x_true) is computed exactly, so it is invisible to the chi-square residual test by construction). A benign hard-negative case (`physical_load_disturbance`, 25-70% load step) is included specifically to be confusable with an attack.

**The `prior_only` feature set, and exactly how its prior is constructed.** Every replication starts from one randomly sampled base operating point, shared across that replication's 4 scenarios. Before any attack/disturbance is applied, an independent copy of that same base point has its loads perturbed by a fresh, independent forecast error (N(0, 2.5%) per load at 5-bus, clipped to ±6%; N(0, 5%) at IEEE 14-bus/30-bus — a cross-network noise-model difference documented as a limitation below) and solved through its own power flow — this is the "prior." `prior_only` features are the deviation between the WLS estimate and this prior. The prior is constructed *before*, and entirely independently of, whichever scenario the true network becomes.

**Features.** Four feature sets: `residual_only` (conventional WLS residuals); `prior_only` (prior-deviation features above); `residual_plus_prior` (the two combined, no relational information at all — the genuinely topology-free ablation); `topology_fusion` (`residual_plus_prior` plus explicit relational features describing each bus relative to its network neighbors).

**A feature-labeling bug, and why `residual_plus_prior` exists.** This project's first pass compared `topology_fusion` only against `residual_only`/`prior_only` individually — never their union, the actual topology-free control. Building that control surfaced a real bug: `residual_only`/`prior_only` were originally defined by column-name substring match ("residual", "innovation"), but relational columns are themselves named after the quantities they compare across neighbors (e.g. `neighbor_mean_residual`) — so the same substring match silently pulled a large share of every relational column into what were meant to be topology-free baselines (5-bus: 30 of 65 relational columns into `residual_only`, 35 of 65 into `prior_only`). The practical effect: `residual_only` ∪ `prior_only`, as originally defined, equaled `topology_fusion` exactly, column for column. Fixed by excluding columns matching relational-name markers (`neighbor_`, `incident_edge_`, `local_minus_`, `local_over_`) and adding their corrected union as the fourth, genuinely topology-free `residual_plus_prior` set.

**A second 5-bus bug, caught later, in a subsequent audit: structural metadata in a "topology-free" baseline.** Independently of the bug above, this project's 5-bus *localization* feature sets carried a further problem, found only once this paper's central finding was audited a second time (§7): every localization feature set — including `residual_only`/`prior_only`/`residual_plus_prior` — was defined with node degree and four device-role one-hot flags (`role_pcc`, `role_pv_gfl`, `role_load`, `role_bess_gfm`) prepended unconditionally. Degree is graph topology by definition; `role_load` is worse, since the attack generator targets only load buses, so this flag is close to handing the localizer the eligible-target set directly, in every feature set tested. Fixed by removing this metadata from all four feature sets, including `topology_fusion` (this structural/role metadata was never what this paper means by "topology-relational" — the explicit neighbor/incident-edge features already cover that). §4 reports only the corrected numbers.

**Models.** LogisticRegression, RandomForest, and HistGradientBoosting, with replication-group-safe 70/15/15 train/validation/test splits and group-aware bootstrap confidence intervals. Model selection uses the validation split exclusively: each candidate is scored on validation, the best-scoring model per feature set is selected, and only that model's (until then unseen) test score is reported. An earlier version of this pipeline selected the reported model by test-set score directly — test-set leakage caught and fixed in the same audit that found the two feature-set bugs above (§7).

**Sample-size terminology.** A *replication* is one independently sampled base operating point; each yields exactly 4 *scenarios* (clean, naive-attack, stealth-attack, physical-disturbance), so N replications yield 4N scenarios. The 70/15/15 split is applied at the replication level. The headline 5-bus result uses N=500 replications → 2000 scenarios → ≈300 test scenarios; §7's IEEE 14-bus/30-bus results use the same N=500 → 300 test scenarios for detection, 150 for localization, at both networks.

## 4. Results I: The Topology-Fusion Advantage Does Not Survive Its Own Ablation, at Either Task

**An initial near-perfect result was a methodology artifact, not a real effect.** The first evaluation of `topology_fusion` reported 1.000 on every metric, across three model families, on a 72-scenario test set. Treated as a red flag: a defensive coding gap was found and fixed (didn't change the result) — the true cause was that the original attack scenarios had large, obvious state bias.

**Under a realistic attack magnitude, `topology_fusion` beat `prior_only` — a real, multi-seed-confirmed, but misleadingly-framed gap.** Reducing attack magnitude ~3x and evaluating at n=300 gave `topology_fusion` a clear lead over `prior_only` alone: detection 0.950 vs. 0.873, localization 0.980 vs. 0.780, confirmed across 4 seeds. All true and reported — but incomplete: `prior_only` is missing residual information `topology_fusion` already has, so this gap couldn't distinguish "topology helped" from "having residual information at all helped."

**The fair comparison, after both 5-bus bugs above are corrected, is a near-exact tie on both tasks.**

**Table 1 — topology_fusion vs. the corrected, topology-free ablation (best validation-selected model per cell; 5-bus, n=300 detection / n=150 cyber-only localization test scenarios)**

| Condition | Detection: topo. fusion | Detection: res.+prior | Localization: topo. fusion | Localization: res.+prior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hard | 0.950 | 0.950 | 0.967 | 0.967 |
| Harder (+2x noise) | 0.953 | 0.953 | 0.967 | 0.967 |

Both tasks are exact ties in both conditions — neither is bolded, since there is no winner. This is a change from an earlier pass (before the second 5-bus bug was found), which reported localization as a topology-free *win* (0.980 vs. 0.993); once degree/device-role metadata are removed from every feature set, that asymmetry disappears entirely. For reference, both feature sets still comfortably beat `prior_only` alone (0.853-0.860 detection, 0.647-0.673 localization) and `residual_only` alone (~0.69 detection, ~0.55 localization) — the gain over either single baseline is real and large; it is simply not a topology effect.

![Topology_fusion vs. the topology-free residual+prior ablation vs. prior_only alone, across 4 seeds](figures/paper_fig1_multiseed_advantage.png)

**The null result replicates across independent random seeds, on both tasks.** Repeating full data generation for four independent seeds gives a `topology_fusion`-vs-`residual_plus_prior` detection gap of +0.003±0.010 (range -0.007 to +0.017) and a localization gap of +0.005±0.036 (range -0.033 to +0.053, including two exact ties): both cross zero and change sign seed to seed — noise around zero, not a real effect on either task. This is a materially different result from an earlier corrected pass (before the degree/role-metadata bug was found), which reported a small, direction-consistent localization gap favoring `residual_plus_prior` (mean -0.003±0.007, never favoring topology); that asymmetry is gone once the comparison is genuinely fair on both sides.

**A structural explanation for the null result.** Predicting each of the 65 relational columns from `residual_plus_prior` alone, with 5-fold cross-validated ridge regression, gives a mean out-of-fold R² of 0.950 (median 1.000, minimum 0.390, 88% of columns above 0.8): a neighbor-mean or incident-edge aggregate over a fixed, small neighbor set is, on a network this size, close to an affine function of the same per-bus values `residual_plus_prior` already provides in full. A sufficiently flexible classifier can reconstruct nearly all of that relational information internally without ever being given it explicitly — consistent with Table 1's exact ties on both tasks. This explanation is specific to a fixed, small, low-redundancy topology, and does not by itself predict what happens at larger, standard networks (§7), where §7 finds exactly two real effects, one per task.

## 5. Results II: Real-Time Feasibility — Diagnosing and Fixing the Gap

Following [4]'s finding that fast classifiers can sit inside a slow end-to-end pipeline, we measured the full per-cycle decision pipeline against a one-cycle (20ms at the network's confirmed 50Hz) target, with one-time setup excluded.

**Initial measurement**: median 42.98ms, 2.15x over budget (RandomForest, tolerance=1e-7, maximum_iterations=40, flat init every cycle).

**The first hypothesis (slow convergence from a cold start) was wrong.** Warm-starting produced only a 1.06x speedup (37.14ms).

**Profiling found an even split between two causes, only one of them algorithmic.** The measurement-submission step cost 19.9ms — essentially the same as the estimator's own solve (19.4ms). An iteration-count ablation showed the true requirement is ~4-5 steps regardless of the 40-iteration cap.

**The measurement-submission cost was pure, fixable software overhead.** The measurement table was rebuilt from scratch every cycle via a general-purpose API, even though its structure never changes. A one-time construction + in-place overwrite gave a 1158x speedup on this sub-step (19.4ms → 0.017ms), verified bit-identical (max difference 0.0).

**Combined with a lighter classifier** (LogisticRegression), the pipeline reaches, at N=300 trials with 0 convergence failures: **16.91ms median, 17.59ms p95, 18.41ms p99, 81.41ms max** — median/p95/p99 within the 20ms budget; a single outlier still exceeds it, traced to the state estimator's own solve-time variability, the same phenomenon a smaller earlier outlier (21.3ms) was already attributed to. Feature computation costs only 0.087ms median, next to 16.63ms for state estimation and 0.19ms for inference — state estimation, not the feature set or classifier, is what the budget is spent on. **An earlier version of this benchmark trained its classifier on synthetic random data and computed its residual from the simulator's ground-truth state**, the same category of problem found and fixed elsewhere in this paper (§7); the classifier is now trained on 150 real warm-up scenarios generated through the same simulated pipeline (83 attack, 67 clean; training balanced accuracy 1.000), and the residual/innovation features use the same deployable quantities as everywhere else. Re-measuring after this fix reproduces the original timing conclusion almost exactly, as expected: a fixed-size computation takes the same wall-clock time regardless of whether the weights/values behind it are real or synthetic — the fix changes what the benchmark represents, not how long it takes.

**Positioning.** [4] measures the same category of gap on an industrial EMT simulator and recommends hardware acceleration; this paper's finding suggests pipeline profiling deserves consideration first. A knowledge-distilled LightGBM detector for virtualized microgrids reports ~0.06ms/sample for inference alone [22] — the same order of magnitude as this paper's own inference sub-step (~0.19ms/sample), nearly two orders of magnitude below the state-estimation sub-step.

## 6. Results III: The Advantage Does Not Extrapolate to Unseen Attack Types

To test generalization beyond the two trained attack families, we trained HistGradientBoosting — with the same tuned hyperparameters as the reported 5-bus detector elsewhere in this paper; an earlier version of this test used an unconfigured default model instead — with one entire family withheld, then measured recall on that family, evaluated only against the standard test split (an earlier version of this evaluation compared the held-out condition against validation-plus-test rows while the seen-in-training reference used test rows only, an asymmetric comparison caught and fixed in the same audit as §7's other corrections). Run for both `topology_fusion` and `residual_plus_prior`.

**Table 2 — Recall on a withheld vs. trained-on attack type (n=75 test scenarios per cell, both conditions)**

| Attack type | Features | Withheld | In training |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naive corruption | topo. fusion | 0.000 | 0.907 |
| Naive corruption | res.+prior | 0.000 | 0.907 |
| Stealth FDIA | topo. fusion | 0.013 | 1.000 |
| Stealth FDIA | res.+prior | 0.013 | 1.000 |

![Recall collapses when an attack type is withheld from training](figures/paper_fig3_zeroday_generalization.png)

The detector recognizes attack types in its training data very well but does not extrapolate to a mechanism it has not seen, identically whether or not topology-relational features are included — the two feature sets are indistinguishable on every cell. **This result is essentially unchanged from an earlier, uncorrected pass of the same test (0-4% withheld recall there vs. 0-1.3% here) — one of the few findings in this paper that a rigorous audit left standing rather than revised.** We report this as a hard scope limit: within this project's own two-family taxonomy, this is a complete absence of zero-day capability, not a partial one. This complements a related gap in the fault-classification literature: an XGBoost classifier trained/evaluated on the *same* distribution still drops from 96.8% (synthetic) to 85.2% (physical) data [23] — even without an unseen attack type, a synthetic-to-real shift alone costs over 11 points.

## 7. Results IV: Cross-Network Evaluation Reveals Task- and Network-Specific Effects

Everything in §4-§6 uses the custom 5-bus microgrid. We separately tested explicit topology-relational features at a larger, standard scale, using a completely independent, size-agnostic feature-engineering design.

**Test-system selection.** CIGRE European MV was tried first and rejected (auxiliary-bus indexing mismatch breaks this project's admittance-matrix code). IEEE 14-bus and IEEE 30-bus have no such mismatch (confirmed directly) and are among the most commonly used benchmarks in the state-estimation/FDIA literature.

**Method.** All WLS/measurement/attack machinery is network-size-agnostic and reused unmodified. Fixed per-bus feature columns were replaced with a size-agnostic design: global summary statistics of the residual/innovation arrays, plus a trained per-node classifier for localization.

**Two independent instances of the same feature-leakage bug — and one entirely different bug found only in a second audit pass.** This feature design, separately implemented from §3's regex-based split, turned out to need an analogous correction: `residual_only`/`prior_only` included the single-argmax-node's neighbor-comparison context (detection) and every node's neighbor-mean/-max/local-minus-neighbor/degree columns directly (localization) — an explicit list inclusion this time, not a substring collision, but the same structural problem. Fixed the same way as §3.

**A second, later audit of this same pipeline found something categorically different and more serious**: the "residual" feature at both IEEE 14-bus and IEEE 30-bus was computed as |x̂ - x_true| — the simulator's hidden ground-truth state, never available to a real detector — instead of the deployable measurement residual z - h(x̂) that the shared state-estimation code already computes and that the 5-bus pipeline used throughout. The same audit found that model selection at both networks (and, independently, at 5-bus) picked whichever of three candidate models scored best on the test set directly, not a held-out validation split. Both are fixed throughout this section. All numbers below are from this twice-corrected pipeline, re-run in full at N=500 replications, four independent seeds, at both networks.

**Result: each standard network shows a real, 4-seed-robust advantage in exactly one task, and it is a different task at each network.** At IEEE 14-bus, the detection gap changes sign across seeds (mean +0.002±0.013) — null, matching 5-bus. The localization gap does not: positive in all 4 seeds (mean +0.018±0.021), never favoring the non-relational alternative — a real, seed-robust advantage, though a noisier one than an earlier, since-superseded pass reported (that version's oracle-leaked residual and test-set-selected models gave a similar-looking mean with roughly half the standard deviation; the direction survived, its precision did not). At IEEE 30-bus, the pattern inverts entirely: the detection gap is positive in all 4 seeds (mean +0.039±0.032) — a real, seed-robust advantage, roughly twice the magnitude of IEEE-14's localization effect and not present in any earlier pass of this analysis. The localization gap at IEEE-30 changes sign across seeds (mean +0.008±0.018) — null, matching 5-bus, unchanged in character from an earlier pass that also found it null.

**Interpretation: three independently-audited pipelines agree that topology-relational features are not a free win, but disagree on where and for which task they pay off.** §4 (5-bus) and this section (IEEE 14-bus, IEEE 30-bus) were built with unrelated feature-engineering code, and all three separately needed the same category of feature-leakage correction, followed by a second, independent category of correction (oracle-leaked residual and test-set model selection, caught only in a later, more adversarial audit). With everything corrected, the honest result is: *detection* shows no reproducible advantage at 5-bus or IEEE-14, but a real one at IEEE-30; *localization* shows no reproducible advantage at 5-bus or IEEE-30, but a real one at IEEE-14. Neither task is uniform across networks, and the two real effects that do survive do not co-occur at the same network or match any single earlier hypothesis this project raised along the way ("topology helps everywhere," "topology helps nowhere," "topology helps localization specifically, and only at larger networks" — each was, in turn, this project's own best reading of the evidence at some point before further replication or auditing overturned it). That the same category of leakage mistake recurred independently in two pipelines, and a more serious category (oracle information reaching a feature meant to be deployable) was caught only on a second, more adversarial pass, is itself informative: neither is reliably caught by result-level scrutiny alone — both were found by reading the feature-computation code line by line against "could a real, deployed detector actually compute this." This sharpens a 2025 field-wide scoping review's [20] independent finding that cross-topology generalization is essentially untested across the literature it surveyed.

## 8. Discussion

Read together, §4-§7 converge on a finding that is uniform for neither task, reached across three networks and pipelines that each needed two separate rounds of correction. For *detection*: no reproducible advantage at 5-bus (an exact tie) or IEEE 14-bus (a null, sign-unstable gap) — but a real, 4-seed-consistent advantage at IEEE 30-bus (gap +0.039±0.032, never negative). For *localization*: no reproducible advantage at 5-bus (an exact tie, once the degree/role bug is fixed) or IEEE 30-bus (a null, sign-unstable gap) — but a real advantage at IEEE 14-bus (gap +0.018±0.021, never negative). Put plainly: each of the two standard IEEE test systems shows a genuine, replicated topology advantage in exactly one of the two tasks, and it is a *different* task at each network; the small custom 5-bus microgrid shows no advantage in either task, on either of two independent bugs' worth of correction.

What helps detection at 5-bus, considerably, is combining residual and prior-innovation information — not a topology-specific effect. §4's redundancy analysis gives the 5-bus null results (now on both tasks) a structural footing: mean out-of-fold R² = 0.950, so there is little left for an explicit relational feature to contribute on a fixed, small topology. The natural hypothesis — that this redundancy weakens on a bigger, more meshed network, explaining IEEE-14's localization reversal — is directly testable, so we tested it: repeating the identical out-of-fold check on IEEE-14's own relational localization features gives a mean R² of 0.966 (median 0.999, 92% above 0.8) — if anything *higher* than the 5-bus figure, not lower. **The redundancy hypothesis does not explain the IEEE-14 localization reversal; linear information content is not the mechanism.** We have not run the equivalent check for IEEE-30's detection-relevant relational features — unlike the localization case, where the same node-level design transfers directly between 5-bus and IEEE-14, IEEE-30's detection features and 5-bus's use different, non-comparable representations, and building a third variant of this diagnostic was judged out of scope. Whether redundancy explains, or fails to explain, IEEE-30's detection advantage is accordingly an open question, not a tested one — we are explicit that we do not know the answer.

A more likely explanation for the IEEE-14 case, though still not directly verified, is discoverability rather than availability: a tree-based model with no built-in notion of "neighbor" must find the right subset of a flat input to combine for each node, a search that may get harder with more nodes even when the information is linearly present throughout. This remains genuinely open, and the picture is now more complicated than a single "discoverability scales with size" story would predict: IEEE-30 is larger than IEEE-14 by node count, yet its localization story (if that is what IEEE-14's is) does not extend to it at all — IEEE-30's own localization gap is null — while IEEE-30 instead shows an advantage in the *other* task, detection, which 5-bus and IEEE-14 both show no advantage in. A mechanism predicting discoverability difficulty scaling smoothly and uniformly across tasks does not fit this pattern; identifying what does is future work.

The paper's original framing — "does topology help, and where does that stop" — was itself built on an assumption (that features and model selection throughout were deployable and unbiased) that turned out to be false by construction in three separate places, not disproven by measurement; the more important finding is not a scale boundary on a single real effect, but a genuinely task- and network-specific pattern with two distinct real effects and no uniform story connecting them, caught only by building the specific ablations a skeptical reviewer would ask for, then auditing the resulting pipeline a second time with the same skepticism turned on our own already-corrected code. We think this pattern — check the suspiciously good result, build the control condition it was never tested against, then go back and check whether the control condition itself was actually fair, more than once if necessary — is itself a methodological point worth stating explicitly, and a cheap one: both audit passes required no new data collection, only reading existing feature-computation code against what a real, deployed detector could and could not observe.

## 9. Limitations

1. **Scale and network-specificity**: Three network topologies have now been tested to the same 4-seed standard, twice-corrected. Detection shows a reproducible advantage at exactly one (IEEE-30); localization at exactly one, different, network (IEEE-14); neither task shows an advantage at 5-bus. None uses a real (non-synthetic) network or load profile, and with only three networks tested, the emerging pattern (each standard IEEE system favoring a different task) is a two-data-point observation, not something a fourth or fifth network is guaranteed to extend in either direction.
2. **Cross-network measurement-noise and forecast-uncertainty mismatch**: measurement standard deviations are fixed absolute values shared, unscaled, across all three networks despite their very different physical scales, and prior load-forecast uncertainty is 2.5% at 5-bus vs. 5% at IEEE-14/IEEE-30 (§3). This was not standardized or swept as an independent robustness axis; §7's cross-network comparisons should be read with this confound explicitly in mind, not as a controlled scale-only experiment.
3. **No zero-day generalization** (§6): numbers hold only for attack types represented in training — verified for both feature sets, which collapse identically, under a corrected, symmetric evaluation protocol; localization was not separately re-tested for zero-day generalization, and the attack taxonomy is still limited to two families (item 4).
4. **Single testbed family, two attack types**: the present AC-consistent attack generator does not independently vary the compromised-measurement subset; introducing explicit attacker-access constraints as a separate, tunable parameter is left for future work.
5. **Latency results** (§5) characterize one Python/`pandapower` implementation on one machine, not a hardware/RTOS claim, and were not re-benchmarked on the smaller `residual_plus_prior` feature set (argued, not re-measured, not to change the conclusion).
6. **Related-work verification is partial**: of ~57 sources surveyed, 41 were read past search-summary level; two invented/misattributed figures and one unverified qualitative claim were caught and corrected during that process, grounds for treating any remaining unstarred source as provisional.

## 10. Conclusion

Across a small custom inverter-dominated microgrid and two standard, larger IEEE test systems (14-bus and 30-bus), each independently and repeatedly audited for feature-leakage, oracle-information, and test-set-selection bugs, explicit topology-relational features show no uniform advantage for either detection or localization. What they show instead is a genuine, replicated, but strictly task-and-network-specific pattern: a real detection advantage at IEEE 30-bus only, and a real localization advantage at IEEE 14-bus only — both confirmed across four independent seeds each, both absent from every earlier, less-audited pass of this same analysis. We take this as a caution against generalizing any single network's result into a general rule from one or two data points, and as the outcome of two full rounds, within this same project, of an unreplicated or under-audited result not surviving the next round of scrutiny. None of the three positive-sounding stories this project entertained along the way (topology helps at 5-bus; topology helps localization specifically at scale; topology's 5-bus null generalizes cleanly to larger networks) survived being checked against a wider, more adversarial reading of the pipeline's own code — the result that did survive is more complicated, and, we think, more genuinely informative than any of them. Separately, the resulting decision pipeline — now verified with a classifier trained on real scenarios and deployable features throughout — can be brought within a one-cycle protection-relevant computational target once its actual bottleneck is found and fixed, and the detector does not extend to attack types unseen in training, confirmed under a corrected, symmetric evaluation protocol. We think the corrected, considerably-more-complicated version of this result is more useful, and more publishable, than any single cleaner-looking story an earlier, less adversarial pass would have supported: it identifies a specific methodological trap (comparing an information-rich feature set, or a model-selection procedure, against a baseline that was not actually fair or deployable) that recurred independently across three separately-written pipelines and two separate audit passes, and it shows that even once such a trap is found and fixed once, checking whether it recurs elsewhere in the same codebase is worth doing before treating any single pass's result as final.
